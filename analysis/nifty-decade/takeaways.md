# Nifty 50 over ten years: price, P/E and earnings (September 2016 to September 2026)

Data to 28 September 2026. Companion files: `nifty_decade_price_pe_earnings.xlsx` (data, formulas and native Excel charts) and `nifty_decade_price_pe_earnings.png` (the chart).

## The one thing to remember

A P/E ratio tells you how many rupees investors pay for each rupee of a company's profit. NSE publishes one for the Nifty 50 every day: the index level divided by the profit its companies reported over the last four quarters. Dividing the index by that P/E gives the profit behind each unit of the index. (Think of the Nifty as one share; this is the profit that one share earned over the past year.)

Over the last ten years, the Nifty 50 went from 8,611 to 22,780, which is 2.6 times. The profit behind each unit of the index grew by about the same amount, 2.7 to 2.8 times, once you allow for a change NSE made in 2021 to how it counts that profit. So investors today pay slightly less for each rupee of profit than they did in September 2016. The more useful part is how the decade got there, because it happened in two halves that worked in opposite ways.

## Takeaways

**1. From 2016 to 2019, prices rose because investors paid more, while profits barely moved.**
Between September 2016 and December 2019, the Nifty rose 41%, from 8,611 to 12,168. The profit behind each unit rose only 17%, from ₹368 to ₹430. The difference came from the P/E, which climbed from 23.4x to 28.3x. Other analysts saw the same drought at the time: PrimeInvestor wrote in December 2019 that Nifty earnings had grown "barely 2 per cent a year" over five years, and ICICI Direct puts Nifty earnings growth at 2% a year from FY17 to FY20. Even the September 2019 corporate tax cut, which lifted Nifty earnings about 10% almost overnight according to Business Standard, only took them to ₹430 by December.

**2. From 2020 to 2026, profits did the work and the P/E came down.**
Between December 2019 and September 2026, the Nifty rose 87%, from 12,168 to 22,780. Over the same stretch, the profit behind each unit grew 2.3 to 2.4 times on a like-for-like basis. For both to be true, the P/E had to fall, and it did: from roughly 23.5x to 25x in December 2019 (restated to today's basis) to 19.3x now, a drop of 18% to 22%. Motilal Oswal's own tally of Nifty earnings shows the same upswing, with growth of more than 20% a year from FY20 to FY24.

**3. NSE changed how it counts profit in 2021, and that breaks most long-run comparisons.**
Until March 2021, NSE worked out the Nifty's P/E using each company's standalone profit, the profit of the parent company alone. From 31 March 2021, it switched to consolidated profit, which also counts subsidiaries. Consolidated profit was about 20% higher that day, so the P/E dropped from 40.4x to 33.2x overnight while the index barely moved. That is why the chart shows the earlier years in a lighter shade. It also means the "10-year average P/E" numbers quoted in the press, which range from 23x to 27x, mix the two methods and make today's market look cheaper than it is. On the consolidated method alone, the median month-end P/E since April 2021 is 22.0x. Today's 19.3x is 12.5% below that, and it is the lowest month-end reading since the switch; the previous low was 19.5x in June 2022.

**4. Like-for-like, the Nifty has been cheaper than today only twice in ten years.**
Restating the pre-2021 P/Es on today's basis, the only months cheaper than today's 19.3x were the weeks after demonetisation in November and December 2016 (about 18x to 19x) and the COVID crash in spring 2020 (about 16x to 17x in March). Both were moments of panic. Two episodes are not enough to say what happens next, so the useful reading is narrower: the market is near the bottom of its ten-year range on this measure.

**5. The trailing P/E and the forward P/E answer different questions.**
NSE's P/E divides today's price by the profit already reported, so it stands at 19.3x. The Bloomberg forward P/E in our earlier chart divides the same price by the profit analysts expect over the next twelve months, so it stands at about 16.9x. The gap between the two, about 14%, is the profit growth analysts are counting on. The trailing number rests on facts; the forward number rests on forecasts, and those forecasts have been cut by about 9% over the past year.

**6. Gains that come from a rising P/E can be taken back; gains that come from profits stay as long as the profits do.**
The first half of the decade shows the first kind. Investors paid 21% more per rupee of profit by 2019 than in 2016, and that extra has since been given back. The second half shows the second kind: the index is higher today because the companies in it earn more than twice as much. Dividends are on top of all this. The Nifty figures here are the price index; the total-return index, which reinvests dividends, grew about 1.35 percentage points a year faster over the ten years to July 2026 (12.39% against 11.04%).

## What this means for a conversation with a client

For a client who asks whether the Nifty is expensive, the decade gives a clear answer on today's numbers. Investors are paying about 19 times the profit these companies actually reported, below the 22x typical of the last five years and close to the lowest level of the decade on a like-for-like basis. The ten-year gain came from profits more than doubling. A low P/E describes the price today and makes no promise about the next few months, since P/E readings have gone lower before, in 2016 and 2020.

## How much to trust these numbers

- NSE's month-end P/E values come from Downstox, which republishes NSE Indices data. 26 of them were checked against NSE's own monthly Index Dashboards and Nippon India Mutual Fund's daily notes, and the largest gap was 0.08, which is rounding. The 28 September 2026 value (19.26) comes from Nippon India's daily note citing NSE.
- The Nifty closes come from Yahoo Finance and were copied twice independently; every one of the 2,484 days matched. One missing day, 29 March 2019, was filled from Yahoo's monthly table.
- The like-for-like figures depend on how much bigger consolidated profit was than standalone profit before 2021. That gap was not constant: about 14% on average over the ten years to 2020 and about 16% in December 2020 on Bloomberg data, and 20.5% on NSE's switch day. Every like-for-like number here is given as a range across that spread.
- As an independent check, Motilal Oswal's count of actual Nifty earnings per share went from ₹393 in FY16 to ₹1,065 in FY26, 2.7 times, inside the 2.7x to 2.8x range above.
