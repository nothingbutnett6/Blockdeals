# Nifty 50 price, forward P/E and earnings since 2020: takeaways

Data to 28 September 2026. Companion files: `nifty_pe_price_earnings.xlsx` (data, formulas and native Excel charts) and `nifty_pe_price_earnings.png` (the chart).

## The one thing to remember

A P/E ratio tells you how many rupees investors pay for each rupee of profit. The version in Bloomberg's chart, the forward P/E, divides the Nifty's level by the profit analysts expect its companies to earn over the next twelve months.

At the low point circled on Bloomberg's chart, in early June 2020, the Nifty 50 traded at about 17 times those expected earnings. On 28 September 2026 it traded at about 17 times again. In between, the index went from 9,979 to 22,780, which is 2.3 times, and the expected earnings behind each unit of the index went from about ₹588 to about ₹1,345, also 2.3 times. (Think of the Nifty as one share priced at 9,979 and later at 22,780; the rupee figures are the profit expected on that one share.) The whole rise in price since then has been matched by the rise in expected profit, so investors today pay the same amount for each rupee of expected earnings as they did a few months after the COVID crash.

## Takeaways

**1. "Cheap" describes the P/E, and the price can be high at the same time.**
Price equals expected earnings multiplied by the P/E. When Bloomberg says Indian stocks are the cheapest since 2020, it means the P/E is low: investors are paying less for each rupee of expected profit than at any time since 2020. The Nifty itself is more than twice its June 2020 level, and it is as cheap as it was then because expected earnings grew as fast as the price did.

**2. This year's fall came from investors paying less for each rupee of profit, while expected earnings kept rising.**
From its closing high of 26,328.55 on 2 January 2026 to 22,780.25 on 28 September, the Nifty fell 13.5%. Over the same months, the twelve-month earnings forecast rose about 9%. For both to be true, the forward P/E had to fall from about 21.3 times earnings (written 21.3x) to 16.9x, a 20% drop in what investors will pay for each rupee of profit.

Foreign selling is one visible source of pressure. Foreign portfolio investors had taken out a net ₹2.45 lakh crore from Indian shares in 2026 by mid-September, more than the ₹1.66 lakh crore they withdrew in all of 2025. Domestic institutions such as mutual funds and insurers bought a net ₹5.8 lakh crore over roughly the same period. Coverage of the late-September slide also points to Brent crude near $108 a barrel, US 10-year bond yields near 5.2%, and a heavy supply of shares from block and bulk deals (₹3.63 lakh crore by late August, according to Prime Database).

**3. Each time price ran well ahead of earnings, the gap later shrank.**
In the top panel, the purple price line pulled away from the orange earnings line three times: into October 2021 (forward P/E about 23x), into September 2024 (about 21.5x) and into November 2025 (about 21.5x). The 2021 gap closed by mid-2022, as prices fell 17% while expected earnings rose 12%. The 2024 gap only narrowed: prices fell while earnings stood still, taking the P/E down to about 18x by March 2025, and then a rally pushed it back to 21.5x. The 2026 fall closed that gap. Today the two lines are as close together as they were in June 2020, even though both are far higher. Since mid-2020 the forward P/E has moved between roughly 17x and 23x, and it now sits at the bottom of that range.

**4. Actual earnings roughly doubled, and the growth came in bursts.**
Motilal Oswal's figure for the profit actually earned per unit of the Nifty went from ₹539 in FY21 (the year to March 2021) to ₹1,065 in FY26, roughly double in five years. Most of that came in FY22, FY23 and FY24, with growth of about 37%, 9% and 25%. After that, earnings grew only about 1% in FY25 and about 5% in FY26. The stall shows in the chart: expected earnings barely moved between September 2024 and December 2025 (up about 1%), and the Nifty went nowhere over the same stretch (26,216 to 26,130). With the P/E already near the top of its range, at about 21.5x in September 2024, flat earnings left the market with little to push it higher.

**5. "Cheap" rests on forecasts, and those forecasts have been cut.**
Expected earnings are analysts' forecasts. The average forecast for Nifty profit per unit in FY27 fell from ₹1,357 to ₹1,235 in the year to May 2026, a cut of about 9%, and JM Financial puts the cut at 9.3% over the year to August 2026. Forecasts steadied in August after strong June-quarter results, when Nifty company profits rose 18% on the year.

Why did the chart's expected earnings still rise in 2026? Our reading is that it is mostly a calendar effect. The chart always looks twelve months ahead, so as 2026 went on, the lower FY26 profits dropped out and more of FY28, where forecasts are higher, came in. Using Motilal Oswal's forecasts of ₹1,232 for FY27 and ₹1,425 for FY28, a half-and-half mix gives about ₹1,329, close to the ₹1,345 in the chart.

This matters because small changes in forecasts move the P/E a lot. If forecasts are cut by another 10% while the Nifty stays at 22,780, the forward P/E rises from 16.9x to about 18.8x, and the market stops looking cheap without any change in price.

**6. "Cheapest since 2020" is Bloomberg's call, and it holds by a whisker.**
On our reading of Bloomberg's chart, today's 16.9x is only 0.1x to 0.3x below the lows of June 2022 (about 17.0x) and March 2023 (about 17.2x). That gap is smaller than the margin of error from reading values off a chart. Motilal Oswal's own series shows the same picture: its September 2026 reading of 17.6x matches its end-June 2022 reading of 17.6x. The fair description is that the Nifty is back at the bottom of the range it has held since mid-2020. For scale, the COVID panic took the forward P/E to about 11.5x in late March 2020, the lowest point on Bloomberg's line, so the bottom of the recent range is no guarantee of how low it can go.

## What this means for a conversation with a client

For a client unsettled by this year's 13% fall, two facts help. First, the profit analysts expect Nifty companies to make over the next twelve months is higher than it was in January, though mostly because that twelve-month view now reaches further into FY28; forecasts for FY27 itself have been trimmed. Second, the fall came from investors paying less for each rupee of those profits. A low P/E tells the client the market is priced at the low end of its recent range against today's forecasts. Whether prices fall further depends on what happens to those forecasts and on how much investors are willing to pay, and they have paid far less before: about 11.5 times earnings in March 2020.

## How much to trust these numbers

- The forward P/E is Bloomberg's series, read off the Bloomberg chart image because Bloomberg does not publish it openly. The rebuilt series was checked against seven forward P/E figures that news reports attributed to Bloomberg and agreed within 0.4x each time (average gap 0.23x). Two other Bloomberg-attributed figures are far off, Mint's 25.5x for September 2020 (the chart shows about 20x) and a secondary report of 22.8x for September 2025 (the chart shows about 20x), and probably come from a different Bloomberg measure. Treat every P/E here as accurate to about ±0.4x.
- The June 2020 comparison uses a brief low. The P/E was near 17x for only a few weeks; by early July 2020 it was back near 20x, in line with what Business Standard and Nomura reported at the time. No independent report from early June 2020 confirms the 17x level. The multiples also depend on the start date: measured from 30 June 2020, the Nifty is up 2.2x and expected earnings 2.4x.
- Expected earnings in the chart are worked out from the other two numbers: the Nifty's closing level divided by the forward P/E, averaged over a month to remove day-to-day noise. Actual reported earnings roughly doubled from FY21 to FY26 (₹539 to ₹1,065, per Motilal Oswal). The rest of the 2.3x rise is still a forecast: today's ₹1,345 is close to a half-and-half mix of the FY27 and FY28 estimates, so it holds only if those estimates hold.
- P/E levels differ by provider because each uses its own forecasts. Motilal Oswal, for example, puts the Nifty at 17.6x in September 2026. Compare P/E levels only within one provider's numbers.
- The Nifty figures here leave out dividends, so they understate what an investor actually earned.
