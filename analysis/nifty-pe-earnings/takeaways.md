# Nifty 50 price, forward P/E and earnings since 2020: takeaways

Data to 28 September 2026. Companion files: `nifty_pe_price_earnings.xlsx` (data, formulas, native Excel charts) and `nifty_pe_price_earnings.png` (the chart).

## The one thing to remember

In early June 2020, the Nifty 50 traded at about 17 times the earnings analysts expected over the next twelve months. On 28 September 2026, it traded at about 17 times again. In between, the index went from 9,979 to 22,780, which is 2.3 times, and those expected earnings went from about ₹588 to about ₹1,345 per index unit, which is also 2.3 times. The whole rise in price since June 2020 is matched by the rise in expected profit. Investors today are paying the same price for each rupee of earnings that they paid in June 2020, a few months after the COVID crash.

## Takeaways

**1. A P/E ratio has two moving parts, and "cheap" only describes one of them.**
Price equals earnings multiplied by the P/E. When Bloomberg says Indian stocks are the cheapest since 2020, it means the multiple is low. It says nothing about the price being low. The Nifty is more than twice its June 2020 level and is as cheap as it was then, because earnings grew just as fast as the price did.

**2. This year's fall came from the multiple, while expected earnings kept rising.**
From its closing high of 26,328.55 on 2 January 2026 to 22,780.25 on 28 September, the Nifty fell 13.5%. Over the same months, forward earnings rose about 9%. For both to be true, the forward P/E had to drop from about 21.3x to 16.9x, a 20% cut in what investors will pay per rupee of profit. The selling behind that shows up in the flow data. Foreign portfolio investors have taken out a net ₹2.45 lakh crore from Indian equities in 2026 so far, more than the ₹1.66 lakh crore they withdrew in all of 2025, while domestic institutions bought a net ₹5.8 lakh crore up to mid-September.

**3. Each time price ran well ahead of earnings, it later came back toward them.**
In the top panel, the purple price line pulled away from the orange earnings line three times: into October 2021 (forward P/E about 23x), into September 2024 (about 21.5x) and into late 2025 (about 21.5x). Each time, the gap later closed, mainly because prices fell back. In 2022 and again in 2026, rising earnings helped close it too; in early 2025, earnings were flat and the price did all the work. Today the two lines sit together at about the same point they did in June 2020. Since mid-2020, the Nifty's forward P/E has moved between roughly 17x and 23x, and it is now at the bottom of that range.

**4. Earnings growth has been lumpy, and the stall shows up in the chart.**
Motilal Oswal's count of actual Nifty earnings per share went from ₹539 in FY21 to ₹1,065 in FY26, roughly double in five years. Most of that came between FY22 and FY24. FY25 grew about 1% and FY26 about 5%. You can see the stall in the chart: forward earnings barely moved between September 2024 and December 2025 (up about 1%), and the Nifty went nowhere over the same stretch (26,216 to 26,130). When earnings stop growing, a market that is already expensive has little to push it higher.

**5. "Cheap" rests on forecasts, and those forecasts are being cut.**
Forward earnings are analysts' estimates, and the consensus estimate for FY27 Nifty EPS has fallen from ₹1,357 to ₹1,235 over the past year, a cut of about 9%. Forward earnings in the chart still rose during 2026, mostly because a twelve-month forward estimate slides along the calendar: as 2026 went on, more of the window fell in FY28, where the estimates are higher. The sensitivity matters here. If estimates are cut by another 10% and the Nifty does not move, the forward P/E goes from 16.9x to about 18.8x, and the market stops looking cheap without a single rupee of price change.

**6. "Cheapest since 2020" is true, but only by a whisker.**
On the Bloomberg series, today's 16.9x sits within 0.1x to 0.2x of the lows in June 2022 (about 17.0x) and March 2023 (about 17.2x). That gap is inside the error of reading a chart, so a fairer description is that the Nifty is back at the bottom of the range it has held since mid-2020. For scale, the COVID panic took the forward P/E to about 11.5x on 23 March 2020, so the bottom of the recent range gives no guarantee about how low the market can go.

## What this means for a conversation with a client

For a client unsettled by this year's 13% fall, the useful fact is that the profits Nifty companies are expected to earn are higher than they were in January. The fall came from the price investors are willing to pay for those profits. That price has been lower before (March 2020), and the profit forecasts themselves can still be cut, so a low P/E is a statement about value today and gives no signal about timing.

## How much to trust these numbers

- The forward P/E is Bloomberg's series, read off the Bloomberg chart image because Bloomberg does not publish it openly. The reading was checked against seven forward P/E figures that news reports attributed to Bloomberg, and it agreed within 0.4x every time (average gap 0.23x). Treat every P/E here as accurate to about ±0.3x.
- Forward P/E numbers differ by provider. Motilal Oswal, for example, put the Nifty at 17.6x in September 2026. Compare levels within one series only.
- Forward earnings in the chart are implied: Nifty close divided by forward P/E, smoothed over a month. They move in line with Motilal Oswal's actual reported earnings (roughly 2x from FY21 to FY26), which is the independent check that the growth is real.
- The Nifty here is the price index, without dividends.
